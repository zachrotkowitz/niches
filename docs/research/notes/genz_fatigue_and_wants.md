# Gen Z Social Media Fatigue, Well-Being Concerns, and What Gen Z Wants Instead

Context: notes to validate or challenge the "Niches" thesis (a phone-first app with separate niche feeds such as Cooking, Art, Photography and Gym; chronological; no attention-bait; built around connection, self-improvement and inspiration). Research date: Nov 3, 2026. Gen Z here means people born about 1997–2012. Many findings below come from secondary coverage because pewresearch.org and fortune.com could not be fetched from this session (network allowlist). Where a figure is secondhand, that is noted.

---

## 1. Dissatisfaction with Instagram, TikTok and Facebook: time spent, wanting to quit, detox, and feeds that no longer show friends

### Takeaway
The evidence is consistent: Gen Z is ambivalent about the big platforms, and much of it is regretful. Many say they spend too much time on them, that they regret it, or that they would prefer the platforms not exist. Behavior moves more slowly than attitudes. Usage is easing globally, but US usage is still high, and most young people say they would struggle to quit. Meta's own trial evidence shows that friend content is now a small share of time on Instagram and Facebook, which supports the "my feed isn't my friends anymore" complaint.

### Cited Findings
**Time spent and wanting to cut back**
- Pew (survey of 1,391 US teens aged 13–17 and their parents, Sept–Oct 2024, published Apr 22, 2025): 45% of teens say they spend too much time on social media, up from 36% in 2022. 48% say social media has a "mostly negative" effect on people their age, up from 32% in 2022. — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/22/teens-social-media-and-mental-health/); [CNN coverage](https://www.cnn.com/2025/04/22/tech/teens-social-media-mental-health)
- GWI data (about 250k adults in 50+ countries, analyzed by the Financial Times in Oct 2025): global time on social media peaked in 2022. By end-2024 average daily use was about 2h20m, 10% below 2022. The decline is steepest among teens and 20-somethings. **The US is an exception:** usage there kept rising and was about 15% above Europe in 2024. The share of people who use social platforms "to stay in touch with friends," "express themselves" or "meet new people" has fallen by more than a quarter since 2014. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/10/07/has-social-media-finally-peaked-the-rise-of-ai-and-decline-of-screen-time); [MediaCat](https://mediacat.uk/social-media-consumption-peaked-in-2022/) (both secondhand reports of the FT/GWI analysis)
- Contradicting data: the UK IPA Touchpoints release reported that mean daily time with social media rose nearly 20% year on year. The two studies use different methods. — [the7stars](https://www.the7stars.co.uk/blog/has-social-media-usage-really-peaked/)
- A 2025 YouGov figure says 47% of US under-30s are trying to reduce screen time, versus 32% of older Americans. The same coverage says 16% of adult Gen Zers own a flip phone or "dumbphone." — [Gen Z Tea newsletter, secondhand](https://genztea.beehiiv.com/p/offline-is-the-new-luxury) (I could not reach the primary YouGov source. Treat as unverified.)
- A survey of 2,000 Americans found 63% of Gen Z say they are "digitally detoxing," the highest of any generation. — [Movieguide, secondhand](https://www.movieguide.org/?p=606904)

**Wanting to quit, deleting apps, and regret**
- Deloitte UK Digital Consumer Trends 2025 (4,150 UK adults aged 16–75, April 2025; Gen Z defined as 16–29): 29% of Gen Z deleted a social media app in the past 12 months, versus 20% of all consumers. The top reasons were too much time spent (25%), misinformation (21%) and negative mental-health effects (20%). More than half of Gen Z support a social media ban for under-16s. Deloitte described this as "digital fatigue." — [Deloitte UK press release](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/gen-zs-favour-social-media-ban-for-under-16s-as-digital-fatigue-hits.html)
- Check My Insurance survey (US, 2025): 52% of Gen Z tried to quit social media in 2025, versus 33% of all respondents. Mental health was the top reason. — [YourTango, secondhand](https://www.yourtango.com/self/survey-says-over-half-gen-z-tried-quit-social-media-2025) (commercial sponsor, methodology not checked)
- Harris Poll of 1,006 US Gen Z adults (**2024**): the share who regret each platform being invented was X/Twitter 50%, TikTok 47%, Snapchat 43% and Facebook 37%, versus only 15% for YouTube. — [The Reboot](https://readreboot.com/p/gen-z-regrets-social-media)
- UK survey of more than 1,700 people aged 16–24: 62% say social media does more harm than good, 70% say they would struggle to quit, and 50% regret the time they spent online growing up. — [SafeScreens](https://safescreens.org/we-grew-up-with-it-we-regret-it-gen-z-calls-out-the-harms-of-social-media/) (advocacy group, so treat with caution)
- British Standards Institution survey of UK 16–21-year-olds (**2024**): nearly half say they would rather live in a world without the internet. — [Cybernews](https://cybernews.com/news/genz-survey-internet-addiction/)
- **"Collective trap" evidence:** Bursztyn, Handel, Jimenez and Roth (NBER WP 31771, 2023; **older data**) ran an incentivized experiment with US college students. Students wanted about $59 to deactivate TikTok and $47 to deactivate Instagram if their peers stayed on. But they would *pay* $28 (TikTok) and $10 (Instagram) to have everyone, themselves included, deactivate. Counting spillovers to non-users, 64% of active TikTok users and 48% of active Instagram users get negative welfare from the products existing. The authors attribute the pull largely to FOMO. — [NBER/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/31771.html); [Marketplace](https://www.marketplace.org/2023/10/10/new-research-quantifies-why-you-want-to-quit-social-media-but-cant/)
- A 2026 *Journal of Public Economics* paper by UNSW researchers ("Bad Networks") makes a related argument: participation is harmful, but opting out carries a higher social cost. — [Medianet](https://newshub.medianet.com.au/2026/04/how-do-social-media-platforms-trap-users-in-networks-they-would-rather-leave/146658)

**Feeds no longer show friends**
- A Meta chart shown at the FTC antitrust trial (April 2025) reportedly showed the share of time spent viewing content from "friends" falling over two years: from 22% to 17% on Facebook and from 11% to 7% on Instagram. — [scribe.disroot.org, citing a New Yorker report](https://scribe.disroot.org/post/2868786/4943954) (secondhand)
- Reuters reported a slightly different version of the same point: Zuckerberg testified that about 20% of content on Facebook and 10% on Instagram now comes from users' friends rather than interest-based accounts. — [Reuters via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/meta-trial-5-key-moments-152043553.html)
- Harris Poll (March 2026, reported by Fortune): Gen Z is already "nostalgic" for an older TikTok. 41% of Gen Z say they miss when there were fewer ads and brands. One in three say they now have to actively "train" their algorithm to see content they want. — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/10/gen-z-nostalgic-for-old-tiktok-more-into-youtube-harris-poll/) (seen through a search summary only; the article could not be fetched)

### Inferences
- A good summary of the market is "want to leave, can't leave." Dissatisfaction is high, but network effects and FOMO keep people in place (Bursztyn). A new app probably can't win by asking people to quit Instagram. It has to win a narrower job (hobby inspiration and progress) where it doesn't need the user's whole social graph.
- The friend-content collapse (single digits on Instagram) and the 41% who miss fewer ads support Niches' "no attention-bait, see what you follow" positioning. Note, though, that Meta's argument is that users chose discovery over friends.
- US users are still using these platforms more and more, so "fatigue" is an attitude rather than a mass exodus in the US.

### Gaps
- Could not fetch Pew's full report (pewresearch.org blocked), so the platform-level 2024–2025 Pew numbers for teens who use each platform "almost constantly" are not included here.
- No primary 2025–2026 US survey found that measures "intent to quit Instagram/TikTok" by platform. The platform regret data is the 2024 Harris Poll.
- The Medium and blog claims that Instagram public story posting by 16–24s fell 29% while private story posting rose 40% (attributed to TechCrunch 2025) could not be verified and are excluded.

## 2. Mental-health evidence and the scientific disagreement

### Takeaway
Official bodies (the US Surgeon General) and Haidt's *The Anxious Generation* argue that social media is a meaningful cause of youth mental-health harm. Prominent researchers (Candice Odgers, Andrew Przybylski, Peter Etchells, Chris Ferguson) argue the evidence shows small, mixed or correlational associations. Teens themselves increasingly *perceive* harm, especially to peers, sleep and productivity. Parents worry more than teens do.

### Cited Findings
- **US Surgeon General Advisory (May 2023; older):** nearly 95% of 13–17-year-olds use social media, and more than a third use it "almost constantly." Adolescents spending more than 3 hours a day on social media face double the risk of poor mental-health outcomes such as depression and anxiety symptoms. The figure is based on a JAMA Psychiatry study. — [Becker's Hospital Review](https://beckershospitalreview.com/public-health/surgeon-general-social-media-health-risks-warrant-warning-labels.html); [VOA](https://www.voanews.com/amp/us-surgeon-general-says-social-media-apps-need-warning-labels-/7659722.html)
- **June 17, 2024:** Surgeon General Murthy called in a NYT op-ed for a tobacco-style warning label on social media. That would need Congress to act. Murthy conceded a label alone would not be enough. Consumer Reports' policy counsel said warning labels "by themselves [are] just not effective." — [Becker's](https://beckershospitalreview.com/public-health/surgeon-general-social-media-health-risks-warrant-warning-labels.html); [Rise Health](https://www.risehealth.org/insights-articles/surgeon-general-calls-for-warning-label-on-social-media)
- **Pew 2025 (teens 13–17, fielded fall 2024):** 48% say social media has a mostly negative effect on people their age (32% in 2022). Teen girls are much more likely than boys to say it hurts their *own* mental health (25% for girls). 34% at least sometimes get mental-health information on social media. Parents are more worried than teens: 55% of parents are extremely or very concerned about teen mental health versus 35% of teens. 44% of parents name social media as the top negative influence on teen mental health, versus 22% of teens, who also point to bullying (17%) and pressures and expectations (16%). — [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/22/teens-social-media-and-mental-health/); [CNN](https://www.cnn.com/2025/04/22/tech/teens-social-media-mental-health); [Washington Post](https://www.washingtonpost.com/lifestyle/2025/04/25/teens-mental-health-social-media/)
- Pew coverage also reports that about half of teens say social media harms their sleep and productivity or focus. — [FingerLakes1 summary](https://www.fingerlakes1.com/2025/04/22/social-media-teen-mental-health-2025/) (secondhand. Exact percentages not verified against Pew.)
- **The Haidt vs. critics debate (2024):** Odgers' *Nature* review argues the book confuses correlation with causation. She says hundreds of researchers have searched for large effects and found "a mix of no, small and mixed associations," and suggests factors like economic hardship may be the real drivers. Haidt responds that experimental evidence exists, including studies where participants cut back on social media, and that Odgers' alternative explanation doesn't fit the timing. Etchells calls the screen-time data "inconclusive." Przybylski and Ferguson question Haidt's policy proposals. Ferguson says there is "no empirical evidence" one of them helps. — [Platformer](https://platformer.news/anxious-generation-jonathan-haidt-debate-critique/); [3 Quarks Daily (Haidt's response)](https://3quarksdaily.com/?p=252281); [Gradient Flow summary](https://gradientflow.com/haidt-and-odgers/)

### Inferences
- The two sides agree on more than it seems: heavy, compulsive, late-night, comparison-driven use is the concern, and *design* matters. That supports Niches' approach of finite chronological feeds, no infinite engagement loops and creative or progress content. Niches should not claim to "fix teen mental health," because the science is contested.
- The gap between teens and parents matters for go-to-market. Parents and regulators are the worried party, so a "well-being by design" story may persuade parents and schools more than teens.

### Gaps
- No direct 2024–2026 data from Common Sense Media or the APA was collected (searches were not run in time). The APA's 2023 health advisory on social media use in adolescence exists but its specifics were not verified here.
- No meta-analytic effect sizes (e.g., Orben/Przybylski) were pulled.

## 3. What Gen Z says it wants: control, chronological feeds, niche communities, close friends, hobbies, authenticity, fewer ads

### Takeaway
The evidence for "fewer ads and brands," "community-driven features" and "control over my feed" is decent. **The evidence for chronological feeds specifically is weak and partly contradictory.** Young users say they prefer algorithmic feeds over chronological ones. What they seem to want is *agency* (steering what they see), not necessarily reverse-chronological order.

### Cited Findings
- **Chronological vs. algorithmic (challenges the thesis):** YouGov (US) found 31% of 18–29-year-olds prefer chronological feeds versus 43% who prefer algorithmic ones. Preference for chronological feeds is strongest among older adults and light users. Frequent users are more likely to prefer the algorithm. — [YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55416-frequent-social-media-users-are-more-likely-to-prefer-the-algorithm-to-chronological-feeds)
- **Control:** A Brave Bison study found 70% of Gen Z say they "direct the algorithm," a higher share than millennials, Gen X or boomers. — [Mediashotz](https://mediashotz.co.uk/users-are-taking-back-control-of-their-social-feeds-study-finds/)
- **Algorithm frustration:** 1 in 3 Gen Z say they have to actively "train" their algorithm to see what they want (Harris Poll, March 2026). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/10/gen-z-nostalgic-for-old-tiktok-more-into-youtube-harris-poll/)
- **Fewer ads:** 41% of Gen Z miss when there were fewer ads and brands (Harris Poll, March 2026). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/10/gen-z-nostalgic-for-old-tiktok-more-into-youtube-harris-poll/)
- **Community features:** A Match Group (YUZU) report found 65% of Gen Z want social media to focus on community-driven features. — [WFLA](https://www.wfla.com/bloom-tampa-bay/bloom-relationships/gen-z-moving-towards-real-life-connections-in-2025/) (sponsored by a dating company, so treat with caution)
- **Policy and design work on "better feeds":** Georgetown's Knight-Georgetown Institute (March 2025) published "Better Feeds: Algorithms That Put People First," which recommends feeds optimized for users' long-term stated preferences rather than short-term engagement. — [KGI PDF](https://kgi.georgetown.edu/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/Better-Feeds_-Algorithms-That-Put-People-First.pdf) (not read in full)
- **Hobbies as connection:** YPulse (Sept 2025; 1,500 US and Canadian 13–39-year-olds) found 87% of 13–17s and 92% of 18–24s have a hobby. YPulse describes Gen Z hobbies as "relationship maintenance tools" as much as personal pursuits. — [YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2025/10/20/gen-zs-top-10-biggest-hobbies-right-now/)
- A May 2025 survey of 1,600 Americans aged 18–28 found most have at least one "grandma hobby" (crochet, journaling, gardening, puzzles). The top reasons were fun (23.8%) and mental health (22.1%). — [SheKnows](https://www.sheknows.com/parenting/articles/1234891015/gen-z-grandma-hobbies/)
- A 2024 survey by the Chinese social app Soul found Gen Z make 5.8 new friends through hobbies on average. — [IPS News](https://ipsnews.net/business/2024/11/09/a-survey-conducted-by-soul-zhanglus-team-to-unveil-how-gen-z-treats-and-views-hobbies/) (China sample, sponsored, 2024)

### Inferences
- **Challenge to the thesis:** "chronological" on its own is not a proven Gen Z demand. Heavy young users say they prefer algorithmic feeds. Niches should pitch "you're in control / follow-only / finite feed" and consider an optional "discover" lane inside each niche, rather than making strict reverse-chronological order the headline.
- **Support for the thesis:** Gen Z's demand for fewer ads, community features and hobby-based connection fits Niches' niche-feed model well. Hobbies already work as social glue for them.

### Gaps
- No high-quality US survey found on Gen Z demand for "self-improvement" or "inspiration" content as a feature, or on "authenticity" metrics (e.g., BeReal-era data from 2024–2026).
- No data on willingness to pay for ad-free social products among Gen Z.

## 4. Trends: private sharing, interest-based communities, hobby culture, offline movements, gym culture

### Takeaway
Public posting is giving way to private, small-circle sharing (DMs, close friends, group chats), though most specific statistics come from weak blog sources. Interest-based platforms are winning Gen Z: Pinterest says Gen Z is now 57% of its users and its fastest-growing cohort. Phone-free and offline clubs are growing but small.

### Cited Findings
- **Pinterest (Q3 2026 earnings call, late Oct 2026):** Gen Z is 57% of users and the "most engaged cohort across monthly retention, saves and sessions," and the fastest-growing cohort. Gen Z ARPU is comparable to millennials'. Two-thirds of Gen Z users say they come to shop. Pinterest has 661M MAU (+21M quarter over quarter) and revenue of $1.174B (+12%). For comparison, Pinterest said Gen Z was "over 40%" of users in mid-2025 and more than 50% by Q1 2026. — [Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/02/pinterest-pins-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/); [MediaPost](https://www.mediapost.com/publications/article/419167/pinterest-builds-out-ad-stack-reports-user-boost.html)
- **Private sharing (directional, weakly sourced):** Many blog posts describe Gen Z moving to Close Friends lists, group chats, locked accounts and invite-only Discords. They attribute to Instagram head Adam Mosseri the claim that more sharing now happens in DMs and Stories than in the feed. — [Medium (unverified)](https://medium.com/@ifso_59790/why-gen-z-is-ditching-the-instagram-grid-for-private-sharing-in-2025-593e80d3575c) (specific percentages unverified)
- The GWI/FT finding that "staying in touch with friends" and "self-expression" motives fell by more than a quarter since 2014 supports the idea that public feeds are no longer the place for social sharing. — [Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/10/07/has-social-media-finally-peaked-the-rise-of-ai-and-decline-of-screen-time)
- **Offline and phone-free movement:** The Offline Club (Amsterdam, 2021) has expanded to 19 cities and has about 600k Instagram followers. London attendees paid £9.50 for phone-free events (Feb 2025). — [Fortune](https://fortune.com/europe/2025/02/20/londoners-pay-for-chance-to-switch-off-their-phones-connect-with-real-world-gen-z-digital-detox). Axios (Apr 2026) reports grassroots phone-free spaces and digital detox retreats growing, led by Gen Z. — [Axios](https://www.axios.com/2026/04/24/phone-free-spaces-digital-detox-retreats-gen-z). The Log Off Movement is youth-led tech advocacy co-founded in 2020 by Emma Lembke. — [Gen Z Tea](https://genztea.beehiiv.com/p/offline-is-the-new-luxury)
- **Caveat:** commentators note that most data show Gen Z's usage going *up*, not down, which means "offline" is partly an aesthetic trend that lives online. — [Raised on Social Substack](https://raisedonsocial.substack.com/p/going-offline-is-the-latest-gen-z-online-trend)

### Inferences
- Pinterest is the clearest proof that a calmer, interest- and inspiration-led platform can attract and keep Gen Z at scale. It is likely Niches' closest analog and competitor, especially for Cooking, Art and Photography.
- "Offline" and hobby culture point to a possible Niches angle: feeds that push people toward doing the hobby (and toward local or in-person meetups), not endless scrolling.

### Gaps
- No verified 2024–2026 data found on Discord or Reddit Gen Z user growth, Substack demographics, or "romanticizing life" or "75 Hard"/gym-culture prevalence. Searches returned nothing usable. The report writer should not assert numbers for these.
- Strava, Hevy and other fitness-community growth data were not collected.

## 5. Regulatory context, 2024–2026, relevant to a new youth-accessible social app

### Takeaway
Age gating is now a core product requirement, not optional. Australia bans under-16s from "age-restricted social media platforms" (since Dec 10, 2025). The US amended COPPA Rule has been fully in force since Apr 22, 2026. State App Store Accountability Acts (Texas in effect; Utah compliance in May 2027) push age signals through app stores. Federal KOSA/KIDS Act legislation is still unresolved as of Nov 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **Australia:** The ban took effect in December 2025 and covers 10 platforms: Instagram, Facebook, Threads, Snapchat, TikTok, YouTube, X, Reddit, Twitch and Kick. Exempt services include Discord, WhatsApp, Messenger, Roblox, Steam, YouTube Kids and Google Classroom. Fines reach A$49.5M. — [Time](https://time.com/7339762/australia-youth-social-media-ban-under-16-snapchat-meta-tiktok/); [MiniMode](https://minimode.com.au/guides/under-16-social-media-ban). About 4.7M accounts were deactivated in the first days. But an eSafety update (Mar 2026) reportedly found more than 80% of under-16s with prior accounts still had access three months later. — [New Technology](https://www.newtechnology.net.au/review/under-16-social-media-ban-australia-2026). On Mar 31, 2026, eSafety opened investigations into Facebook, Instagram, Snapchat, TikTok and YouTube. — [ABC News](https://www.abc.net.au/news/2026-03-31/five-social-media-platforms-under-investigation-under-16s-ban/106513690). Amendment rules (Mar 25–26, 2026) require a service to have a "recommender feature" and/or "logged-in feature" to be age-restricted. — [New Technology](https://www.newtechnology.net.au/review/under-16-social-media-ban-australia-2026) (secondhand; verify on eSafety)
- **COPPA Rule amendments (US, applies to under-13s):** The FTC published them on Apr 22, 2025. They took effect Jun 23, 2025, and full compliance was due Apr 22, 2026. A key new obligation is separate verifiable parental consent before disclosing children's data to third parties for targeted advertising. A Feb 25, 2026 FTC policy statement limits enforcement when children's data is collected solely for age verification. — [Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches); [Toy Association](https://toyassociation.org/PressRoom2/News/2026-News/updated-coppa-rule-requirements-take-effect-april-22.aspx)
- **App Store Accountability Acts:** Texas SB 2420 (effective Jan 1, 2026) was enjoined, then the Fifth Circuit stayed the injunction, letting it take effect. — [Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1799524/key-developments-with-state-app-store-accountability-acts-as-texas-act-takes-effect). Utah SB 142's compliance deadline was extended to May 6, 2027, and CCIA dropped its challenge on Apr 21, 2026. Louisiana's act was delayed to Jul 1, 2027. — [Mondaq (Utah amendment)](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/technology/1773038/utah-first-state-to-amend-its-app-store-accountability-act); [Stoel Rives](https://www.stoel.com/insights/publications/utahs-app-store-accountability-act-goes-into-effect)
- **Texas SCOPE Act (HB 18):** the Fifth Circuit upheld an injunction blocking part of the law (2-1, July 2026). — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCOPE_Act) (verify the scope with the primary ruling)
- **Federal:** The House passed the KIDS Act (H.R. 7757) on Jun 29, 2026. It folds in parts of KOSA and COPPA 2.0 but drops KOSA's "duty of care." Senate sponsors called it "dead." — [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/house-advances-bipartisan-kids-online-safety-bill-but-senate-showdown-looms); [K-12 Dive](https://www.k12dive.com/news/house-passes-kids-internet-and-digital-safety-act/824128/). The Senate Commerce Committee advanced an amended KOSA (S. 1748) on Aug 5, 2026, with no full Senate vote found. The Senate passed a version of COPPA 2.0 in March 2026. — [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/kosa-privacy-social-media-senate.html); [Wikipedia: KOSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids_Online_Safety_Act)
- Several state laws limiting youth social media access have passed but been blocked in court. — [Becker's](https://beckershospitalreview.com/public-health/surgeon-general-social-media-health-risks-warrant-warning-labels.html)

### Inferences
- The simplest launch posture is 18+ (or at least 16+) with app-store age signals. That avoids COPPA's parental consent regime and Australia's under-16 restriction. Teen users (13–17) would bring state-law and KOSA-style "duty of care" risk.
- Australia's 2026 rules tie restriction to having a "recommender feature." A follow-only, chronological product might be treated differently there, but that is untested and needs legal review.
- An ad-light model also reduces COPPA exposure, since the new third-party targeted-ad consent rule is the main new burden.

### Gaps
- Not verified: Florida HB 3, the status of other state social media age-verification laws, the EU DSA minor-protection guidelines, or the UK Online Safety Act children's codes (in force 2025). The report writer should flag these as needing legal review.
- Whether a follow-only or chronological app falls outside Australia's "age-restricted social media platform" definition after the March 2026 rules was not confirmed.
