# Build Niches as a Hobby Tool First

Niches becomes its best product by working as a useful hobby tool before it works as a social network. That means saved collections and progress goals that are worth using on day one with zero followers, one or two niches launched densely instead of four launched thinly, and humane defaults (a feed that ends, tap-to-play video, private like counts, notifications batched into digests) presented as user control rather than as an "anti-algorithm" crusade. The case for this is strong. Gen Z is tired of the big platforms but stuck on them: **45% of US teens say they spend too much time on social media** ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/22/teens-social-media-and-mental-health/)), and college students would pay to have everyone quit TikTok but demand about $59 to quit alone ([NBER](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/31771.html)). So Niches should not ask people to leave Instagram. It should do one narrower job better, which is hobby inspiration and progress. The history of niche apps sets the base rate. Novelty and values-only apps (BeReal, Poparazzi, Vero, Cohost) spiked and faded. The survivors (Strava, Letterboxd, Pinterest) are tools you log or save into, and they charge for deeper utility. The founder's core feature set is mostly well aligned with that pattern. Three points need adjusting. Strict chronological order is not a proven Gen Z demand. A newest-first feed needs a replacement discovery engine. Instagram and TikTok saves can be imported, but only through user-driven share-sheet links and export files, because no API exists. The demo is cheap: Expo, Supabase and a managed video service cost about **$35–100 a month for 100–500 testers**. The real constraints are founder time, content density and a minimum safety setup that app stores require.

## Gen Z wants control and a narrower job, not another Instagram

The fatigue is real and measurable, but it shows up as an attitude, not as people leaving. Pew found that the share of US teens who say they spend too much time on social media rose from 36% in 2022 to **45%**. The share who say it has a mostly negative effect on people their age rose from 32% to **48%** ([Pew](https://www.pewresearch.org/internet/2025/04/22/teens-social-media-and-mental-health/)). In the UK, **29% of Gen Z deleted a social app in the past year**, mostly because of time spent, misinformation and mental health ([Deloitte UK](https://www.deloitte.com/uk/en/about/press-room/gen-zs-favour-social-media-ban-for-under-16s-as-digital-fatigue-hits.html)). Half of US Gen Z adults regret that X/Twitter was invented, and 47% say the same of TikTok, compared with only 15% for YouTube ([The Reboot](https://readreboot.com/p/gen-z-regrets-social-media)). Global time on social media peaked in 2022, but **US usage kept rising** ([Irish Times on FT/GWI](https://www.irishtimes.com/business/2025/10/07/has-social-media-finally-peaked-the-rise-of-ai-and-decline-of-screen-time)). The best summary is "want to leave, can't leave." The NBER experiment shows why. Students put a high price on deactivating alone but would *pay* $28 to have everyone deactivate TikTok, and 64% of active TikTok users experience the product's existence as a net welfare loss ([NBER](https://ideas.repec.org/p/nbr/nberwo/31771.html)). This is a collective trap held in place by FOMO. A new app escapes it by not needing the user's whole social graph, and hobby feeds don't.

The incumbents have also stopped delivering what they once promised. Zuckerberg testified that only about **20% of Facebook content and 10% of Instagram content now comes from friends** ([Reuters via Yahoo](https://www.yahoo.com/news/meta-trial-5-key-moments-152043553.html)). A March 2026 Harris Poll found that 41% of Gen Z miss when there were fewer ads and brands, and one in three say they have to actively "train" their algorithm ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/03/10/gen-z-nostalgic-for-old-tiktok-more-into-youtube-harris-poll/)). Hobbies are where this generation connects: YPulse finds **92% of 18–24-year-olds have a hobby** and describes Gen Z hobbies as "relationship maintenance tools" ([YPulse](https://www.ypulse.com/article/2025/10/20/gen-zs-top-10-biggest-hobbies-right-now/)). The clearest proof that a calmer, inspiration-led product can hold Gen Z at scale is Pinterest. Gen Z is now **57% of its users** and its most engaged cohort on retention, saves and sessions ([Motley Fool transcript](https://www.fool.com/earnings/call-transcripts/2026/11/02/pinterest-pins-q3-2026-earnings-call-transcript/)). Pinterest is both the model and the main competitor for Cooking, Art and Photography.

One part of the thesis does not hold up: **chronological feeds are not a demonstrated Gen Z preference.** YouGov found that 31% of 18–29-year-olds prefer chronological feeds and **43% prefer algorithmic ones**, and heavy users lean even further toward the algorithm ([YouGov](https://yougov.com/en-us/articles/55416-frequent-social-media-users-are-more-likely-to-prefer-the-algorithm-to-chronological-feeds)). What Gen Z does claim is agency: 70% say they "direct the algorithm" ([Mediashotz](https://mediashotz.co.uk/users-are-taking-back-control-of-their-social-feeds-study-finds/)). Niches should therefore pitch "you choose the niche, you see what's new, the feed ends" rather than leading with "chronological." Mental-health claims need the same care. The Surgeon General links more than three hours a day of use to double the risk of depression and anxiety symptoms ([Becker's](https://beckershospitalreview.com/public-health/surgeon-general-social-media-health-risks-warrant-warning-labels.html)). Researchers such as Candice Odgers counter that the evidence shows "a mix of no, small and mixed associations" ([Platformer](https://platformer.news/anxious-generation-jonathan-haidt-debate-critique/)). Both sides agree that compulsive, comparison-driven, late-night use is the concern and that design matters. Niches can honestly claim well-being *by design*. It cannot claim to fix teen mental health.

## Survivors are tools first, and every fad lost most users within months

Recent niche and "anti-algorithm" apps share a clear pattern. **Apps built on a novelty format collapsed quickly.** BeReal lost 61% of daily users between October 2022 and March 2023 ([Wikipedia: BeReal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeReal)). Clubhouse lost more than 60% of its active users within about six months of its peak ([Medium analysis](https://medium.com/@achu_83391/what-happened-to-clubhouse-a-data-driven-analysis-of-social-medias-fastest-rise-and-fall-7c1ee1386ec9)). Poparazzi went from #1 at launch to shutting down in 2023 ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/01/once-hot-photo-sharing-social-app-poparazzi-is-shutting-down)). **Values-only positioning drives sign-ups but does not pay the bills.** Cohost had a no-ads, no-algorithm manifesto and only **2,630 paying subscribers against roughly 30,000 monthly users** before it shut down from lack of money and founder burnout ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/09/12/cohost-the-x-rival-founded-with-an-anti-big-tech-manifesto-is-running-out-of-money-and-will-shut-down)). Cara grew from about 40,000 to 650,000 users in one week when artists fled Meta's AI training ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2790719)), and then faced a reported **$96,000 cloud bill** and outages while bootstrapped ([Wired mirror](https://wirednews.pages.dev/story/cara-portfolio-app-artificial-intelligence-jingna-zhang)). Vero's ad-free, chronological pitch reversed within days once users found ads and questions surfaced about its founder ([Daily Beast](https://www.thedailybeast.com/as-vero-blows-up-backlash-builds-against-shady-social-app/)). Peach shows the cold-start failure: it had no way to find existing friends, so new users arrived to an isolated experience ([Arizona Wildcat](https://wildcat.arizona.edu/136826/uncategorized/a-month-after-release-is-peach-social-media-app-already-dead/)).

The durable apps share one trait: **they are useful to a single person before any friends join.** Strava logs workouts and makes money from Premium analytics. It has ARR near $500M even though only about 5% of active users pay ([Sacra](https://sacra.com/c/strava/)), and it runs more than 1M clubs ([SQ Magazine](https://sqmagazine.co.uk/strava-statistics/)). Letterboxd logs films and grew to about 30.7M members on subscriptions and partnerships ([StockTitan/Tiny](https://www.stocktitan.net/news/TNYZF/tiny-ltd-announces-corporate-updates-including-board-appointment-wqzh476o11ba.html)). In 2026 it is being shopped at a reported **$250–300M+** valuation ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/industry-news/business/letterboxd-sale-gigi-pritzker-investor-consortium-acquisition/)). Retro, a private weekly photo app, sells richer comments and unlimited history rather than reach ([Fast Company](https://www.fastcompany.com/91462432/retro-photo-sharing-app)). Samsung Food shows the opposite lesson: reviewers say the Whisk acquisition turned a community-focused app into a monetization-heavy one ([Android Authority](https://www.androidauthority.com/samsung-food-3517054/)).

Mapped onto Niches, this is encouraging. **Saved collections (the Pinterest loop) and progress posts with goals (the Strava loop) are the two single-player hooks.** They are the reason to open the app on day three, before anyone follows you. Hidden likes, niche toggles and newest-first order are the stance, and the stance alone carried Vero and Cohost only so far. A daily or weekly ritual can hold a core audience: Voodoo says about half of BeReal's remaining users in its main markets are active six days a week ([Voodoo](https://voodoo.io/news/2025-a-year-of-growth-and-transformation-for-voodoo)). A weekly "progress check-in" prompt inside each niche can borrow that cadence without making novelty the whole product. Two further warnings apply. Never inflate growth: IRL raised about $170M before a board found **95% of its users were bots or automated**, and the SEC charged its founder ([SEC](https://sec.gov/newsroom/press-releases/2024-92)). And incumbents can copy any feature at scale. Threads reached 500M monthly users on Instagram's graph ([Meta](https://about.fb.com/news/2026/06/meta-launching-new-features-500-million-monthly-threads-users/)). Niches' advantage has to be its community and tool depth in specific hobbies, not a single feature.

## Humane design means feeds that end, tap-to-play video and private counts

### What the evidence says about the founder's three signature choices

**Hidden like counts** are defensible, but they are not a proven cure. Instagram tested hiding them from 2019 to 2021 under "Project Daisy." An internal slide reported that "we didn't observe movements in overall well-being measures" ([The Wire](https://thewire.in/tech/facebook-internal-research-hiding-likes-better-for-users-social-comparison)). The test also reduced ad and engagement metrics ([Shorenstein Center](https://shorensteincenter.org/case-study-online-youth-harms-project-daisy/)). In May 2021 Instagram shipped hiding as an opt-in toggle, with likes visible by default ([Good Morning America](https://www.goodmorningamerica.com/news/story/instagram-users-option-hide-likes-77899569)). Mosseri called the idea "very polarizing," because some people posted more without counts while creators missed the metric ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/instagram-says-that-its-exploring-options-that-would-enable-users-to-hide/596253)). Researchers have called hidden likes and hashtag bans "quick technical fixes" that leave the underlying body-image harm untouched ([Western Sydney University](https://researchers.westernsydney.edu.au/en/publications/the-body-image-problem-on-social-media-novel-directions-for-the-f/)). The right model for Niches is Daisy's: counts are hidden from viewers and visible privately to the poster. Pair this with richer feedback that has no public score, such as comments, a one-tap "cheer," and "saved to 12 collections." Also avoid any "top posts" surface that would bring popularity rankings back.

**Newest-first feeds** carry a deliberate engagement cost. In the 2023 *Science* experiment, moving users to reverse-chronological feeds substantially reduced time on the platforms, by roughly 20% on Facebook and 11% on Instagram according to the co-author's summary. It produced no detectable change in political attitudes ([CSMaP NYU](https://csmapnyu.org/research/how-do-social-media-feed-algorithms-affect-attitudes-and-behavior-in-an-election-campaign)). A later critique notes that Meta changed its ranking during the study ([arXiv](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18393)). Instagram and Bluesky both offer chronological views as a choice next to ranked or custom feeds. Bluesky's pinned feeds appear "as separate tabs at the top of their timeline," which is almost exactly Niches' toggle ([Bluesky](https://bsky.social/about/blog/7-27-2023-custom-feeds)). Niches should accept lower minutes and measure saves, return visits and posting instead. It should also remember the user's niche choice. Silently resetting it is the behavior a 2025 complaint under the EU DSA's dark-pattern rules targets at Meta ([GFF complaint](https://freiheitsrechte.org/uploads/documents/Freiheit-im-digitalen-Zeitalter/Center-for-User-Rights/20250414-meta-complaint_redacted.pdf)).

**Stopping cues must be real.** Instagram's 2018 "You're All Caught Up" message keeps older posts flowing right below it ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2018/07/02/instagram-youre-all-caught-up-feature/)). In 2022 Instagram also raised its minimum daily limit from 10 to 30 minutes ([MacRumors](https://www.macrumors.com/2022/02/22/instagram-changes-app-daily-use-limits)). Niches can do better: each niche ends at "You're caught up in Cooking," and older posts sit behind an explicit tap. Notifications have unusually good evidence. In a randomized field trial, **batching notifications three times a day improved attention, mood and sense of control**, while turning them off entirely *raised* anxiety and FOMO ([Fitz et al., author PDF](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/57a40c19414fb54f51f8095f/t/614a55faa7b89e25f4e48ad1/1632261627146/2019+Fitz+Batching.pdf)). So the default should be a digest at times the user picks, with real-time alerts only for direct replies. For video, a randomized Netflix experiment found autoplay had a "substantial impact" on engagement ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.16040v1)). Interviews found it made viewers feel less in control ([UChicago](https://news.uchicago.edu/story/does-netflix-chill-our-free-will-uchicago-computer-scientists-investigate)), and manual play produced the most attentive viewing ([Penn State](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/preventing-users-from-going-down-rabbit-holes-of-extreme-video-co/)). Video should be tap-to-play and sound-off, with no auto-advance and no separate full-screen swipe feed. It should live inside the niche feed and end when the feed ends.

### Dark patterns to design out from the start

Harry Brignull's deceptive.design catalog and the EU DSA's dark-pattern ban give a usable checklist ([deceptive.design](https://www.deceptive.design/types/confirmshaming); [DSA Art. 38 text](https://www.springlex.eu/en/packages/dsa/dsa-regulation/article-38/)). The table pairs the ones most likely to creep into a social app with the Niches alternative.

| Dark pattern | Where it creeps in | Niches default |
|---|---|---|
| Infinite scroll | Feed auto-loads forever | "Caught up" end per niche; older posts behind a tap |
| Autoplay / auto-advance | Next video starts on its own | Tap to play, end card with "Replay" / "Back to feed" |
| Variable-reward pings | Real-time "X liked your post" | Digest at user-chosen times |
| Confirmshaming and nagging | "No thanks, I don't care about my goals"; repeated permission prompts | Neutral "Not now"; ask once, at a moment of clear value |
| Streak loss-aversion | "Your streak will die!" | Private weekly streaks with rest days, no guilt pushes |
| Friend spam | Contact upload that auto-invites | Optional, explicit, never messages contacts |
| Roach motel | Hard to delete account or export | One-screen delete and export, collections included |
| Feed reset | App reverts to a ranked view | Persist niche and feed choices |

Progress and goals need particular care in Gym. Duolingo's data suggests that forgiving streaks with freezes help retention: allowing two freezes instead of one raised daily users by 0.38% ([Duolingo](https://blog.duolingo.com/duolingo-streak-research/)). The pushy notifications are the part to avoid. The bigger risk is body image. In a randomized study of 108 women aged 17–25, fitspiration images raised negative mood and body dissatisfaction compared with travel images, with **no effect on later exercise** ([Flinders University](https://news.flinders.edu.au/blog/2020/03/06/women-deflated-by-fitspo-images/)). Men viewing physique images also reported lower body satisfaction ([OrthoAtlanta](https://www.orthoatlanta.com/health-news/instagram-influencers-making-guys-more-body-conscious)). Gym goal templates should therefore track performance and habits (lifts, consistency, distance) rather than weight. Weight, body-fat and pace numbers should be hideable on progress posts, as Strava does for biometrics ([road.cc](https://road.cc/content/tech-news/strava-adds-privacy-features-hiding-data-285549)). There should be no leaderboards. Apple separately bars "hot-or-not" objectification of real people ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Before/after photos can carry a "body progress photo, tap to view" cover and a setting to hide them. That is an untested inference built from Pinterest's NEDA-developed weight-loss ad ban ([CBS News](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/pinterest-bans-weight-loss-diet-ads)) and Meta's 2024 hiding of eating-disorder content from teens ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/01/09/facebook-instagram-hide-posts-suicide-self-harm-eating-disorders-meta-lawsuits-zuckerberg)).

The screen layout should follow conventions people already know. A five-item bottom bar (Home, Search/Discover, Create, Saved, Profile) keeps navigation visible; hidden hamburger menus reportedly cut discoverability by about 20% ([Smart Insights on NN/g](https://www.smartinsights.com/digital-marketing-strategy/are-hamburger-menus-hidden-navigation-good-for-ux/)). Home gets a scrollable row of niche chips that scales as niches are added. Saved uses a two-column masonry grid. Profile shows niche chips with a grid filtered by niche, plus a Progress tab. Touch targets should be at least 44pt/48dp, which comfortably clears the WCAG 2.2 AA minimum of 24px ([Smashing Magazine](https://smashingmagazine.com/2024/07/getting-bottom-minimum-wcag-conformant-interactive-element-size/)). Alt-text prompts and video captions should be built in from the start.

## Saves import works through share-sheet links and export files, never an API

**No official API returns a user's saved Instagram posts or TikTok favorites.** Instagram's Basic Display API ended on December 4, 2024 ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/slugbye/instagram-data-api-in-2026-what-metas-api-can-and-cant-do-3b0a)). TikTok's Display API scopes cover only a user's own videos ([TikTok scopes](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/tiktok-api-scopes)). TikTok's Data Portability API covers only EEA and UK users and requires an approval that takes about 3–4 weeks ([TikTok](https://developers.tiktok.com/products/data-portability-api/)). Any vendor offering "Instagram saved posts API" access is almost certainly automating logged-in sessions, which both platforms' terms forbid ([ConductAtlas on Meta](https://conductatlas.com/platform/meta/meta-terms-of-service/automated-data-collection-restrictions/); [ConductAtlas on TikTok](https://conductatlas.com/platform/tiktok/tiktok-terms-of-service/prohibition-on-scraping-and-data-extraction/)).

That leaves two legitimate paths, and they fit together. The first is **"Share to Niches" from the share sheet**, the pattern consumer recipe apps already use. Honeydew's flow is simply "In TikTok tap Share → choose Honeydew" ([Honeydew](https://honeydewcook.com/blog/recipe-importing-tiktok-grocery-list)). In Expo, the `expo-share-intent` package handles iOS and Android but needs a development build rather than Expo Go ([GitHub](https://github.com/achorein/expo-share-intent)). Newer Expo docs also describe receiving shares through `expo-sharing`, with a caveat that the iOS approach is not officially supported by Apple ([Expo docs v58](https://docs.expo.dev/versions/v58.0.0/sdk/sharing.md)). What arrives is a public post URL and nothing more. Instagram links carry an `igsh` tracking parameter that should be stripped ([fixupxer](https://github.com/NeatCode-Labs/fixupxer/releases/tag/v2.6.3)). The exact payload on current phones still needs testing on a dev build.

The second path is **bulk import from the user's own data export**, which few consumer apps offer and which would make a strong demo moment. Instagram's export contains `saved_collections.json`, a flat list of collection headers followed by items. Each item has the owner's username, the post URL and the time it was added ([onfabric parser](https://github.com/onfabric/context-use/blob/main/context_use/providers/instagram/saved/schemas.py)). That means an Instagram "Recipes" collection can become a Niches "Recipes" board automatically. The inner keys are localized to the export language, so the parser must not hard-code English labels ([0dot77 notes](https://github.com/0dot77/media-tech-collection/blob/main/CLAUDE.md)). TikTok's `user_data_tiktok.json` holds favorites as a flat list of dates and links. The research found **no evidence that it includes TikTok's named favorites folders** ([no-crumbs corroboration](https://github.com/LiorJossef/no-crumbs/blob/main/docs/evidence/capture/raw/07-export-json-schema-corroboration.txt)). TikTok imports therefore land in an "Imported from TikTok" inbox for sorting. Both exports contain only links. Instagram's can take anywhere from minutes to days to arrive, by third-party accounts ([listsdiff](https://listsdiff.pages.dev/guides/instagram-data-download-time-and-files)). This makes export import a one-time onboarding step, not ongoing sync.

Displaying the imported items is the hard part. Meta removed `thumbnail_url` and `author_name` from Instagram oEmbed on **November 3, 2025** ([Iframely](https://iframely.com/updates/193071-facebook-and-instagram-oembed-thumbnail-deprecation)). Since June 15, 2026, Meta's oEmbed needs no access token, with tokenless calls capped at 1,000 requests per endpoint per hour ([WP Mayor](https://wpmayor.com/meta-tokenless-oembed-wordpress/)). TikTok's public oEmbed still returns a thumbnail ([TikTok docs](https://developers.tiktok.com/doc/embed-videos)), but platform image URLs are signed and expire within days ([Iframely help](https://iframely.com/help/015914-how-to-deal-with-expiring-images-of-tik-tok-and-instagram)). The workable demo design has three parts. TikTok cards use the oEmbed thumbnail, refreshed when needed. Instagram cards use a branded placeholder showing the creator's handle and the user's own note. Tapping any card opens the official embed. The design avoids scraping, media downloads, and re-hosting creators' images on public or shared surfaces. Copying images is the legally riskiest choice, and the exact caching clauses in Meta's and TikTok's terms could not be confirmed. Any shared board shown to other users should use official embeds. Counsel should review this before any caption extraction or thumbnail caching.

## Launch one niche densely, measure retention and ship the safety minimum

### Cold start is solved one niche and one community at a time

Every precedent began with a small, hand-picked group of creators. Andrew Chen calls this group the "hard side" and calls the first self-sustaining community the "atomic network." He also notes that hobbies are a natural source of networks ([Twosapp summary](https://medium.com/twosapp/the-cold-start-problem-how-to-start-and-scale-network-effects-by-andrew-chen-813f0668c70f)). Pinterest started with about 30 design bloggers who handed out invites, and it reached only about **10,000 users nine months after launch** ([Product Habits](https://producthabits.com/how-pinterest-became-an-11-billion-company-by-organizing-the-worlds-hobbies/)). Strava's founders recruited from local cycling clubs ([Stanford eCorner](https://stvp.stanford.edu/wp-content/uploads/sites/3/2024/09/strava-finds-its-niche-transcript.pdf)). Fizz reached **95% of Stanford undergrads** before expanding ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2022/10/04/fizz-app-college-stanford-social/)). BeReal paid campus ambassadors, with a reported $30 referral bonus ([Brown Daily Herald](https://www.browndailyherald.com/article/2022/02/new-social-media-app-tells-students-to-bereal)). Paid seeding alone does not keep users. Lemon8 paid a creator cohort to post before opening to everyone and reached the US top 10, but TechCrunch later reported it had "failed to gain traction," with 2.6M total US downloads ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2023/11/03/lemon8-the-instagram-rival-from-tiktok-parent-bytedance-fails-to-gain-traction-in-u-s)).

For Niches, this points to recruiting 20–50 people who already post one hobby, for example a campus lifting club or a culinary or photo club, and having them post for a week or two before inviting their friends. The founder can openly run a "house" account with prompts and weekly challenges ("show your first loaf"). Reddit's founders, by contrast, filled the early site with fake accounts ([Vice](https://www.vice.com/en/article/how-reddit-got-huge-tons-of-fake-accounts-2/)). That is a trust risk Gen Z journalists now look for. TechCrunch reported that some of BeReal's growth was manufactured ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/?p=2305292)). There is also a design point that is easy to miss. A newest-first feed that shows only people you follow is empty for a new user. At demo scale, **each niche feed should show every post in that niche, newest first**, so the community itself is the discovery engine. A "Following" filter can come later. This is an inference rather than a tested result, but it removes the cold-start weakness that hurt other chronological apps. The same logic covers new niches. The user-request form can collect votes, but a niche should open only once it has seeded creators, because Chen's framework says cold start must be solved again for each new network ([Charter](https://www.charterworks.com/the-cold-start-problem-andrew-chen/)).

### Validation runs on past behavior and cohort retention, not compliments

Rob Fitzpatrick's *Mom Test* rules apply directly: talk about the person's life rather than the idea, ask about specific past behavior, and treat "I would use that" as noise ([mtlynch.io](https://mtlynch.io/book-reports/the-mom-test/)). Good questions include where someone last posted their cooking or gym progress, whether they keep a log, and what they did with their last 20 saved reels. Usability testing should run in rounds of about five users per audience, since five find roughly 85% of problems. Gym users and artists should be tested separately ([NN/g](https://nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/)). Then a closed beta of 30–100 people in one niche over at least four weeks should be judged against a16z's social-app tiers: **OK is D1/D7/D30 = 50/35/20%, Good is 60/40/25%, Great is 70/50/30%** ([a16z](https://a16z.com/do-you-have-lightning-in-a-bottle-how-to-benchmark-your-social-app/)). Because likes are hidden, the beta also needs proxy metrics: weekly posters as a share of weekly actives, saves per user, the share of users who set a goal, time to first post, and the share of posts that get a comment within 24 hours. With fewer than 100 testers, one person moves retention by 1–3 points. The numbers are directional, and the reasons behind them matter as much.

### The trust and safety floor is set by app stores and federal law

A user-generated content app must meet Apple Guideline 1.2 to be listed: filtering of objectionable material, a way to report content with "timely responses," user blocking, and published contact information. Account deletion must be available inside the app (5.1.1(v)) ([Apple](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). Google Play requires social apps to publish standards that prohibit child sexual abuse and exploitation (CSAE) and to name a child-safety contact ([Google Play Help](https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/14747720)). US providers must report apparent CSAM to NCMEC. The 2024 REPORT Act extended the required preservation of reported material to **one year** ([congress.gov](https://www.congress.gov/118/plaws/publ59/PLAW-118publ59.htm)). The tooling is cheap. Cloudflare's CSAM Scanning Tool is free and needs only a verified email ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/a-simpler-path-to-a-safer-internet-an-update-to-our-csam-scanning-tool/)). OpenAI's multimodal moderation model covers text and images ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/upgrading-the-moderation-api-with-our-new-multimodal-moderation-model/)) and is reportedly free, though that was not confirmed. AWS Rekognition costs about **$0.001 per image and $0.10 per video minute** ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/how-to-decide-between-amazon-rekognition-image-and-video-api-for-video-moderation/)). Registering a DMCA agent costs $6 every three years and preserves the copyright safe harbor ([Proskauer](https://www.proskauer.com/alert/copyright-office-establishes-new-electronic-dmca-agent-registration)), which Section 230 does not provide ([CRS](https://www.everycrsreport.com/files/2024-01-04_R46751_9fa55d4913ef4d0ce4a81cb34dedf3333782a277.html)).

Age is the most consequential setting. The amended COPPA Rule has been fully in force since April 22, 2026 ([Hunton](https://www.hunton.com/privacy-and-cybersecurity-law-blog/coppa-rule-amendment-compliance-deadline-approaches)). Australia's under-16 ban took effect in December 2025 ([Time](https://time.com/7339762/australia-youth-social-media-ban-under-16-snapchat-meta-tiktok/)). Texas's app-store age law is in effect ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/unitedstates/privacy-protection/1799524/key-developments-with-state-app-store-accountability-acts-as-texas-act-takes-effect)), and federal kids' safety legislation is still unresolved ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/05/kosa-privacy-social-media-senate.html)). The simplest posture is an **18+ US-only beta**, which a college-campus launch makes natural. The UK's Online Safety Act applies to user-to-user services "large or small" ([Ofcom](https://ofcom.org.uk/online-safety/illegal-and-harmful-content/guide-for-services)), which is another reason to limit store availability to the US for now. The cost is that 13–17-year-olds, a real part of Gen Z, are excluded until legal review. Product choices reduce risk further. Launching without DMs removes the channel where grooming and harassment cluster. Publishing an AI-art policy (either ban it, or require a label with a filter) settles the issue that powered Cara's surge ([80.lv](https://80.lv/articles/anti-ai-art-portfolio-platform-cara-reaches-300-000-users/)). Pro-eating-disorder content cannot be handled by hashtag bans alone: after Instagram's 2012 bans, variant tags grew 15–30% beyond the originals ([TechRadar](https://www.techradar.com/news/internet/if-you-ban-instagram-hashtags-you-ll-only-make-them-stronger-1316612)). Report categories and human review are needed alongside any blocklist.

## Expo and Supabase build a three-platform demo for under $100 a month

For a founder steering an AI coding agent from a cloud Linux machine, **Expo (React Native) plus Supabase** is the clear choice. Expo SDK 57 (June 30, 2026) runs React Native 0.86 on the New Architecture only ([Expo changelog](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-57)). Since SDK 55, Expo has shipped agent skills and an MCP server built specifically for AI coding tools ([Expo changelog SDK 55](https://expo.dev/changelog/sdk-55)). AI assistants have also seen far more React code than Dart, which is Flutter's language ([DEV Community](https://dev.to/krunal_groovy/react-native-vs-flutter-vs-expo-vs-lynx-2026-which-to-choose-for-your-app-30h6)). Expo Router produces iOS, Android and the "web second" version from one codebase. EAS Build compiles iOS in the cloud, so no Mac is needed. EAS Update pushes JavaScript fixes to testers without a new review ([Expo docs](https://docs.expo.dev/develop/development-builds/development-workflows.md)). Native Swift and Kotlin would mean two codebases and a Mac. A web-only app can't fully provide the native share-sheet target that saves import depends on.

Supabase fits because a social app's data is relational: follows, niches, posts, comments, collections and goals. "Newest posts in niche X" is a single indexed SQL query. Hidden likes become a database rule that lets only a post's author read its like count. The free tier pauses projects after a week of inactivity. Pro costs **$25 a month** and includes 100,000 monthly users, 100 GB of storage and 250 GB of egress ([makerkit](https://makerkit.dev/blog/md/saas/supabase-pricing)). Firebase now requires its pay-as-you-go plan for Cloud Storage and has no default spending cap ([Firebase pricing](https://firebase.google.com/pricing); [devtoolpicks](https://devtoolpicks.com/blog/convex-vs-supabase-vs-firebase-indie-hackers-2026)). Video should go to a managed service rather than raw files. Mux has the best developer experience and a free plan (10 stored videos, 100,000 delivery minutes) ([Mux](https://www.mux.com/blog/story/pricing)). Bunny Stream is the cheapest, with free transcoding and per-GB billing ([Bunny docs](https://docs.bunny.net/stream/pricing)). Cloudflare Stream charges $5 per 1,000 stored minutes and $1 per 1,000 delivered minutes ([Cloudflare](https://developers.cloudflare.com/stream/pricing/index.md)). On the front end, FlashList v2 handles feeds, and its `masonry` prop handles Pinterest-style boards ([FlashList](https://shopify.github.io/flash-list/docs/v2-migration)). NativeWind v4 is the stable styling choice; the Gluestack v5 alpha is not ([gluestack discussion](https://github.com/gluestack/gluestack-ui/discussions/3366)).

## Recommendations for the first version, in build order

The demo has two jobs: show the idea convincingly, and find out whether one hobby community comes back without being asked. Everything below serves one of those jobs. "P0" means the demo is not credible without it. "P1" means build it right after P0 works. "Defer" means wait until the beta shows retention.

| Priority | Build | Why |
|---|---|---|
| P0 | Expo + Supabase skeleton; sign-up with 18+ date-of-birth gate; five-tab layout with niche chips | Cheapest path to iOS, Android and web from one codebase |
| P0 | Photo and text posts into one niche; newest-first niche feed showing *all* niche posts, with a real "caught up" end | Core experience; solves cold start at demo scale |
| P0 | Saved collections (create boards, save any post, masonry grid) | Single-player hook #1 (the Pinterest loop) |
| P0 | Goals and progress posts (performance and habit templates, a progress timeline on the profile, hideable stats) | Single-player hook #2 (the Strava loop) |
| P0 | Hidden likes (author-only count) plus comments and a one-tap cheer | Signature stance with replacement feedback |
| P0 | Report, block, in-app account deletion, contact page, terms and community guidelines (CSAE, AI-art and pro-eating-disorder rules), image and text filtering at upload | App-store requirements and basic safety |
| P1 | "Share to Niches" from Instagram and TikTok (dev build), with TikTok thumbnails and Instagram placeholders opening official embeds | The import users will actually use |
| P1 | Instagram export import that maps collections to boards; TikTok favorites to an inbox | High-impact demo moment, done legitimately |
| P1 | Short video (60–90s cap, 720p, tap to play, no auto-advance) on Mux or Bunny | Requested format, kept humane and cheap |
| P1 | Batched notification digest; weekly progress check-in prompt; "request a niche" voting | Retention without dark patterns; demand signal for new niches |
| P1 | Profiles with niche tabs; "Following" filter inside each niche | Unified identity once the network exists |
| Defer | DMs, under-18 accounts, EU/UK availability | Highest safety and legal load |
| Defer | Algorithmic or "discover" ranking, public leaderboards, follower counts | Revisit only with data; never rank bodies |
| Defer | TikTok Data Portability API, Meta oEmbed Read approval, thumbnail caching, AI auto-sorting of imports | Approvals, legal review, and scale not yet needed |
| Defer | Paid tier (advanced goal analytics, unlimited history and collections) | Design it now, charge after retention is proven |

Sequencing matters as much as scope. Build all four niches into the data model and the chip row, because adding a niche costs almost nothing in code. Seed and recruit for **one niche first, two at most**, and show the rest as "coming soon." Gym-plus-campus or Cooking-plus-club cohorts are the most natural fits; Gym and Cooking also pair the two strongest single-player loops (progress logging and recipe saving). Run Mom Test interviews before writing code. Run five-person usability rounds on the web preview and Expo builds. Open the beta only once 20–50 seeded creators have filled the niche. Keep the public promises narrow and keepable. "No ads ever" and "no algorithm ever" are exactly the absolutes that broke Vero. "You choose the niche, the feed ends, likes are private" is a promise Niches can keep at any scale.

## Conclusion

The research changes what Niches' differentiation should be. Hidden likes, newest-first order and a no-attention-bait stance are easy for incumbents to copy and have not, on their own, kept any app alive. What compounds is the tool layer: a collections system that already holds a user's hundreds of scattered Instagram and TikTok saves, and a progress log that turns a hobby into a visible record. A competitor can't copy those quickly, because they hold the user's own history. The share-sheet import is therefore a retention feature, not a convenience: every saved reel deepens the single-player value before the social layer exists.

The main open question is not technical, and the demo is well placed to answer it. Will people post progress where likes are private and the feed ends? Instagram's tests suggest some people post more without counts and others lose interest. Niches' beta should test this directly through the share of weekly users who post, rather than assume it. If a single seeded niche reaches a16z's "OK" retention line with mostly organic posting, the model works and adding niches is a repeatable playbook. If it doesn't, the collections and goals tools still have standalone value as a calmer Pinterest-and-Strava for hobbies. Either way, the founder learns something real for under $100 a month.
